Vibe Coding, Explained

Building software by describing what you want in natural language and letting AI write the code — plus where it helps and where it bites.

Cambrian AI Explainers·September 11, 2026·1 min read

Vibe coding is the practice of building software mostly by describing what you want in plain language and letting an AI generate the code — steering by the "vibe" of the result rather than reading and writing every line yourself. The term was popularized by Andrej Karpathy in early 2025 and has since become shorthand for a new, conversational way of programming.

How it works in practice

You prompt: "build me a landing page with a signup form," look at what the AI produces, then refine — "make the header sticky," "the form isn't submitting, fix it" — iterating by feel. The AI writes and rewrites; you judge outcomes. Modern coding agents extend this by running the code, reading errors, and fixing themselves in a loop.

Why it caught on

It collapses the distance between idea and working prototype. Non-engineers can build tools; experienced developers can move dramatically faster on boilerplate, glue code, and first drafts. For prototyping and small projects, it can feel like magic.

Where it bites

Vibe coding shines for prototypes and stumbles on production. Code you didn't read is code you may not understand — which makes debugging, security, and long-term maintenance harder. Subtle bugs, unhandled edge cases, and quietly insecure patterns can slip in when no human reviews the output.

The pragmatic stance

Treat vibe coding as a powerful accelerator, not a replacement for engineering judgment. Let AI draft; then read, test, and harden what matters — especially anything touching data, money, or users. The developers getting the most from it pair the speed of generation with the discipline of review.
