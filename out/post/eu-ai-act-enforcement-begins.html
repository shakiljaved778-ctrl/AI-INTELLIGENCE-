EU AI Act Enforcement Begins: What Companies Must Do Now

The first obligations under Europe's landmark AI law take effect, and the compliance clock is running.

AI Journal Staff·August 20, 2025·7 min read

Why Editor picked this

“The single most consequential AI regulation in force today — every company shipping AI into Europe needs to read this.”

The European Union's AI Act, the world's most comprehensive attempt to regulate artificial intelligence, has entered its enforcement phase. The first tranche of obligations is now binding, and companies that build or deploy AI systems for the European market face real deadlines.

The law takes a risk-based approach, sorting AI systems into tiers — from banned "unacceptable risk" uses to lightly regulated minimal-risk tools — with the heaviest obligations falling on high-risk and general-purpose systems.

The tiers, briefly

At the top, certain practices are prohibited outright, including social scoring and specific forms of biometric surveillance. High-risk systems — think hiring, credit, and critical infrastructure — carry documentation, testing, and human- oversight requirements. General-purpose models face transparency duties scaled to their capability and reach.

What companies must do now

Legal teams are triaging. The near-term to-do list is concrete: inventory every AI system in use, classify each by risk tier, and document data sources, testing, and intended purpose. Providers of general-purpose models must prepare technical documentation and summaries of training data.

Penalties for the most serious violations run into the tens of millions of euros or a percentage of global turnover — enough to command board-level attention.

The global ripple

As with data privacy before it, Europe's rules are likely to set a de facto global standard. Multinationals rarely maintain two product lines, so compliance built for Brussels tends to travel. Expect other jurisdictions to borrow the Act's vocabulary even where they diverge on substance.

The practical message from counsel is consistent: start now, document everything, and treat classification as an ongoing process rather than a one-time exercise.
