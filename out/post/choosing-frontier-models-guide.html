How to Choose Among the New Frontier Models

GPT-6 Astra, Claude Fable 5.1, Gemini 3.8 Flash, and Meta's new flagship all landed in a week. A practical guide to picking.

Cambrian AI Staff·September 9, 2026·5 min read

Why Editor picked this

“The most useful thing to read after launch week — a calm framework for choosing, not chasing.”

Four frontier models in one week is a lot to evaluate. Here is a practical way to choose without burning a sprint on it.

Start with the workload, not the leaderboard

Public benchmarks are a starting point, never a decision. Pull ten to twenty representative tasks from your own product — the messy, real ones — and run each candidate through the same harness. Score quality, but also latency and cost per successful task, which is what actually shows up on the bill.

Match the model to the tier

Not every call needs the frontier. Google positioned Gemini 3.8 Flash squarely at the cheap-but-capable "workhorse" tier; Anthropic cut cached-input prices on Claude Fable 5.1 to court heavy agentic workloads; OpenAI pitched GPT-6 Astra at the hardest engineering and science problems. Route easy calls to cheap models and reserve the frontier for what genuinely needs it.

Weigh switching costs

A model that's 3% better on a benchmark rarely justifies re-platforming. Factor in migration effort, prompt and tool rewrites, and the reliability you'd give up by leaving a system your team already understands.

Decide, then move on

Pick one or two models you can actually maintain, standardize on them, and re-run your evaluation suite only when something materially changes. The labs will keep sprinting; your job is to be ready, not to keep pace.
