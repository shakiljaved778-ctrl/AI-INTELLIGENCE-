Anthropic Files to Go Public in What Could Be a Record IPO

The maker of Claude has filed to list on the public markets under the ticker ANT, setting up one of the largest — and most hotly debated — technology debuts ever.

Bayaan AI Staff·October 26, 2026·3 min read

Anthropic, the AI lab behind the Claude family of models, has filed to go public — a listing that, depending on where it prices, could rank among the largest technology debuts in history. The company submitted a confidential draft registration statement to the U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission earlier this year and, according to reports this week, has now moved toward a listing expected to trade under the ticker ANT, potentially as soon as November.

The headline number is where the drama lives. Anthropic's last confirmed private valuation was roughly $965 billion, set during a funding round earlier in 2026. Reports on the IPO put the potential public valuation anywhere from around $1.5 trillion to $2 trillion — which would make it one of the biggest offerings ever. At least one skeptical analyst values the company at a mere $150 billion, calling it the "most ridiculous IPO" of the year. That roughly ten-fold spread between bull and bear is, in miniature, the entire argument about AI's value right now.

Why the numbers are so far apart

The disagreement isn't really about Anthropic's technology, which few dispute is among the best in the field. It's about how to value a company growing at extraordinary speed while losing enormous sums of money. Reports describe revenue climbing fast year over year — into the billions — alongside operating losses of a similar or larger scale, the cost of training frontier models and buying the compute to run them.

Optimists see a company compounding revenue faster than almost any business in history, with a product customers clearly want and pricing power still ahead of it. Pessimists see staggering cash burn, fierce competition from equally well-funded rivals, and a valuation that bakes in years of flawless execution. Both are looking at the same filing and reaching opposite conclusions — which is exactly why the pricing will be watched so closely.

What a listing would mean

For Anthropic, going public would convert a company funded by a handful of deep- pocketed private backers into one owned, in part, by public shareholders — bringing quarterly scrutiny, disclosure requirements, and a market that reprices the stock every day. It would also hand the company a new currency for raising the capital that frontier AI devours.

For the wider industry, an Anthropic debut would be a bellwether. The AI buildout has so far been financed largely in private markets and on tech giants' balance sheets; a mega-IPO tests whether public investors will underwrite the thesis at these prices. A strong reception would validate the boom and likely pull other AI companies toward the exits. A weak one would be read as the first real crack in the narrative.

The caveats

Much remains unsettled. As of now the offering price, the number of shares, and the final listing date have not been set, and the valuation figures circulating are reports and estimates, not confirmed terms. Sources even differ on the underlying financials. Until the prospectus is public and the stock actually prices, the precise numbers should be treated as unconfirmed.

What is clear is the significance. Whatever Anthropic ultimately fetches, its debut will be one of the defining financial events of the AI era — a public vote, in real dollars, on how much this technology is actually worth.
