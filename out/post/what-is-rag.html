What Is RAG (Retrieval-Augmented Generation)?

How to give a language model access to your own, up-to-date knowledge — without retraining it.

Cambrian AI Explainers·September 11, 2026·1 min read

A large language model only knows what it learned during training. Ask it about your company's internal policy or yesterday's news and it will either decline or confidently make something up. Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) fixes that by fetching relevant information at question time and handing it to the model as context.

The core idea

RAG splits the job in two:

  1. Retrieve. Search a knowledge source (your documents, a database, the web) for the passages most relevant to the user's question.
  2. Generate. Paste those passages into the model's prompt and ask it to answer using that provided context.

The model still writes the answer, but it's now grounded in real, current, and often private information it never saw in training.

How it works under the hood

Most RAG systems rely on embeddings and a vector database. Documents are chopped into chunks, each chunk is turned into a numeric vector that captures its meaning, and those vectors are stored. At query time, the question is embedded too, and the system finds the chunks whose vectors are "closest" — i.e., most semantically similar — and feeds them to the model.

Why it matters

RAG is the workhorse pattern behind most enterprise AI assistants. It lets a general model answer questions about your data, keeps answers current without expensive retraining, and — crucially — can cite its sources, which makes answers checkable.

The caveats

RAG is only as good as its retrieval. Feed the model irrelevant or wrong chunks and it will faithfully produce a wrong answer. Chunking strategy, search quality, and keeping the knowledge base fresh matter more than the model choice. Done well, RAG turns a clever generalist into a reliable expert on your world.
