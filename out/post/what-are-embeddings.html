Embeddings: How AI Turns Meaning Into Numbers

The quiet building block behind semantic search, RAG, recommendations, and vector databases.

Cambrian AI Explainers·September 11, 2026·1 min read

An embedding is a way of representing meaning as a list of numbers. Feed a word, sentence, image, or document to an embedding model and it returns a vector — often hundreds or thousands of numbers — that captures what that thing is about. It's one of the most useful ideas in modern AI, and it sits under a surprising amount of what you use every day.

Similar meaning, similar numbers

The magic property is this: things with similar meaning get vectors that are close together in space. "Dog" and "puppy" land near each other; "dog" and "tax law" land far apart. Because meaning becomes geometry, you can now do math on concepts — measure how related two texts are by how close their vectors are.

What that unlocks

  • Semantic search — find results by meaning, not exact keywords.
  • RAG — retrieve the passages most relevant to a question.
  • Recommendations — "more like this" for products, songs, or articles.
  • Clustering & classification — group or label content automatically.

A concrete intuition

Imagine every possible sentence placed as a point on a vast map, arranged so related ideas sit near one another. An embedding model is what assigns each sentence its coordinates. A vector database is the map's index, built to answer "what's nearby?" in an instant.

The takeaway

Embeddings are the bridge between messy human meaning and the precise math computers can search and compare. Understand embeddings and most of the modern AI retrieval stack — vector databases, semantic search, RAG — suddenly makes sense as variations on one idea.
