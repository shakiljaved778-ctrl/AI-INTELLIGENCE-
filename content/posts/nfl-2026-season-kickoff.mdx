Football is back. The NFL's 2026-27 regular season kicked off with the **New England Patriots at the Seattle Seahawks**, the traditional curtain-raiser that turns a long, quiet summer into five months of Sundays that matter.

Opening week is a poor predictor and a great mood-setter, and this year's mood is uncertainty — the good kind. After a stretch of seasons defined by one or two obvious favorites, the league enters 2026 looking flatter: a dozen teams with plausible playoff cases, a quarterback class still sorting itself into tiers, and a handful of new coaching staffs whose systems no one has enough tape on yet.

## What to watch

The early weeks are really about separating the pretenders from the contenders on two questions: which offenses have solved their line problems, and which defenses can generate pressure without blitzing. Those are the levers that decide January, and they show up in September.

For fans, the appeal of a wide-open year is simple — more games with stakes, fewer foregone conclusions. The opener rarely tells you who wins it all. But it tells you the season has started, and this one starts with the field looking unusually level.
