Spend enough time with a capable AI model and you notice something disorienting:
it is brilliant and useless in ways that make no human sense. It drafts a nuanced
argument, then miscounts the items in a short list. It writes working code for a
hard problem, then insists a simple fact is true when it plainly isn't. We keep
reaching for human analogies — "it's like a smart intern" — and they keep failing,
because the capability isn't shaped like ours.

Researchers have a useful name for this: the jagged frontier. AI ability is not a
smooth line from easy to hard that tracks our intuitions. It is jagged — spiking to
superhuman on some tasks and collapsing on others that look, to us, no harder.
Tasks that sit inside the frontier get done brilliantly; tasks just outside it,
even nearby ones, get done badly or confidently wrong. And the border is invisible
until you cross it.

## Why this causes so much confusion

Almost every argument about AI is really an argument about which side of the
frontier someone happened to land on. The person who got a dazzling result and the
person who got nonsense are both describing the same system honestly. The hype and
the disappointment are not contradictions; they are two points on a jagged line.

It also explains why benchmarks mislead and why demos oversell. A demo picks tasks
inside the frontier; real work wanders across the border constantly. A tool that is
magic in the showcase can be maddening in the wild, not because anyone lied, but
because the terrain is uneven.

## Using it well

The practical skill of this era is learning the shape of the frontier for your own
work — building the intuition for what to trust, what to verify, and what to not
hand over at all. The people getting real value from AI aren't the ones who believe
it can do everything or nothing; they're the ones who have mapped where it is
strong and keep a firm hand everywhere else.

The frontier is also moving outward fast, which makes the map worth redrawing
often. But the shape stays jagged. The mistake is expecting a tool that is
superhuman at one thing to be merely competent at the next. It won't be — and
knowing that is most of what it takes to use AI like an adult.
