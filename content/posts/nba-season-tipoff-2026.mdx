Basketball is back. As the NBA opens a new season, it does so in a state the league
has not enjoyed in a while: genuinely wide open. There is no single super-team that
everyone concedes the title to, a deep field of contenders separated by thin margins,
and a rising class of young stars ready to take the torch from the generation that
dominated the last decade.

Parity makes for a better watch. When a dozen teams can plausibly imagine a deep
playoff run, the regular season carries stakes it lacks in an era of foregone
conclusions. Every trade, every injury, every hot streak reshapes the picture — and
the sport's month-to-month drama returns.

## The style keeps evolving

On the court, the game continues its long transformation. The three-point revolution
reshaped how teams score; the current frontier is versatility — big players who
handle and shoot, guards who defend multiple positions, and lineups that refuse to be
pinned down. The teams pushing that flexibility furthest tend to be the ones setting
the pace.

## What to watch

Three things will define the season's arc. First, the young stars: which of the new
generation makes the leap from promising to unavoidable. Second, health: in a league
this deep, the contenders who stay intact in spring have an outsized edge. And third,
the chase for fit over talent — the rosters that mesh often outrun the ones that
merely accumulate names.

For fans, a wide-open year is the best kind. The favorites are faint, the newcomers
are electric, and the long road to spring starts with everything still to play for.
Tip-off is here, and for once, almost no one knows how it ends.
