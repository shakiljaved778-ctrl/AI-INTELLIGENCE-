After years dominated by chatbots and copilots, capital is flowing toward
**physical AI** — robotics, industrial automation, autonomous systems, and
defense technology — where models meet the real world.

## Why the shift

Two things make physical AI attractive to investors right now. First, the
software has caught up: advances in perception and learned control make robots
useful in messier, less structured environments. Second, the moat is real —
companies operating physical fleets accumulate proprietary operating data that
is genuinely hard for competitors to replicate.

## Software tied to hard assets

The pattern investors favor is "software wrapped around physical assets":
autonomy stacks, warehouse and manufacturing systems, inspection robots, and the
data platforms that make them smarter over time. Unlike a pure-software feature
that rivals can clone, a deployed fleet compounds an advantage with every hour it
runs.

## The caution

Physical AI is capital-intensive and slow to scale — hardware, safety
certification, and maintenance economics all gate the pace. The winners will be
the ones who pair genuine autonomy with a defensible data loop, not those with
the flashiest demo reel. But the direction of travel is clear: AI is moving off
the screen and onto the factory floor.
