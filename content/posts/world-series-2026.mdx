Baseball has done its annual winnowing. A field that started the postseason crowded
with contenders has been cut, series by nerve-shredding series, down to two: the
last teams standing, bound for the World Series. It is the stage the whole long
season points toward — where 162 games of accumulated evidence gets handed to a
best-of-seven that often ignores all of it.

That is the enduring magic and cruelty of October baseball. The regular season
rewards depth and consistency over six months; the postseason rewards whoever is
hot for two weeks. A team can grind out a historic win total and bow out early to a
scorching pitcher and a few timely swings. The two clubs that reached the final
stage did so not necessarily by being the best all year, but by being the best when
it mattered most — and by surviving the margins that decide elimination games.

## What decides a Series

Short series turn on a few levers. Starting pitching matters enormously when a
rotation can be lined up to throw its best arms on full rest. Bullpens get exposed
or exalted in the late innings of tight games. And the lineups that keep producing
when the pitching sharpens — when every at-bat is contested and runs get scarce —
tend to be the ones hoisting the trophy. Defense and a single momentum-swinging play
can tilt a game, and a game can tilt a Series.

## The stage

There is nothing else quite like it in American sport: a champion decided not in a
single Sunday but across a week or more of games, each one capable of flipping the
story. Momentum is real and fragile; a team down early can roar back, and a team up
can unravel. The pressure compresses careers into single moments — the swing, the
pitch, the catch that gets replayed for decades.

Two teams are left, the long arithmetic of the season behind them. What remains is
the purest version of the sport: a handful of games, everything on the line, and
the knowledge that in October, the best team does not always win — but the one that
does will have earned every inch of it.
