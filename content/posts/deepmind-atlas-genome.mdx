Google DeepMind has published AI-powered predictions for the effect of all **9
billion possible single-letter mutations** in the human genome, using a model
called **Atlas**, built from its earlier AlphaGenome system. It is one of the
most comprehensive attempts yet to use AI to interpret the raw text of human DNA.

Most single-letter changes are harmless; a small number are not. The challenge
has always been telling which is which at scale — a task that would take
conventional labs lifetimes. Atlas aims to compress that search by predicting the
likely consequence of every variant in one sweep.

## From letters to consequences

Where large language models predict the next word, genomic models like Atlas
predict the functional effect of a change to the genetic sequence. Trained on
known biology, the system flags which mutations are likely to disrupt gene
function — a map researchers can use to prioritize which variants to study.

The promise is speed. A ranked atlas of variant effects could help researchers
zero in on the changes most likely to drive disease, rather than searching in the
dark.

## The caveats

A prediction is a hypothesis, not a diagnosis. Every flagged variant still needs
experimental validation, and models trained on existing data can inherit its
blind spots. But as a starting map for the vast, mostly uncharted space of human
genetic variation, releasing predictions for all 9 billion possibilities is a
striking demonstration of AI as an instrument of discovery.
