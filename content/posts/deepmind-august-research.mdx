Ahead of its high-profile genomics work, Google DeepMind spent August publishing
on two of the field's stubborn problems: models that state confident falsehoods,
and models whose reasoning falls apart when a familiar problem is reworded.

## Fewer confident errors

One line of work targeted factual reliability in visual question answering —
reducing the rate at which models give confident but wrong answers about images.
Reliability, not raw capability, is increasingly the frontier that matters for
deployment in high-stakes settings.

## Reasoning that survives reformulation

A second thread addressed "reasoning collapse" — the tendency of models to fail
when a problem they can otherwise solve is presented in an unfamiliar form.
DeepMind reported a training approach that made reasoning meaningfully more
robust to such reformulations on its benchmarks.

## The through-line

Both efforts point the same direction: the next gains are less about scoring
higher on clean benchmarks and more about behaving predictably on messy,
adversarial, real-world inputs — the conditions that actually break systems in
production.
