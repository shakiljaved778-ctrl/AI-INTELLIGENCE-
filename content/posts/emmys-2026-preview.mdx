Television hands out its top honors on **Monday, Sept. 14**, live from the **Peacock Theater** in Los Angeles, with **Mariska Hargitay** — Olivia Benson herself — stepping in as host. The nominations tell a story about where the medium's center of gravity now sits.

Leading the field with a striking **25 nominations** is **"The Pitt,"** the hospital drama that turned procedural grit into prestige. Close behind, **"Hacks"** earned **24 nods** for its fifth and final season, setting a record in the comedy category on its way out — the rare show that peaks as it exits. And Apple TV's debut drama **"Pluribus"** announced itself with **18 nominations**, a stunning haul for a brand-new property.

## The subplot: platform power

Behind the trophies is the streaming endgame. New properties from deep-pocketed platforms are arriving fully formed and awards-ready, while long-running cable franchises make what increasingly look like final stands. The Emmys have become a scoreboard for that shift — a night that rewards the shows, but also ratifies which distributors now set the cultural agenda.

For viewers, the nominations double as a watch-list: a hospital drama, a comedy going out on top, and a buzzy newcomer, all vying under the lights. The winners get the headlines. The pattern of who's nominated tells you where TV is headed.
