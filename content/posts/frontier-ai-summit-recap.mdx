This week's Frontier AI Summit drew lab leaders, regulators, and enterprise
buyers into the same rooms, where the mood swung between genuine optimism and
sober caution. Here are five takeaways worth carrying home.

## 1. Reasoning is the new battleground

Every major lab framed its roadmap around reasoning and reliability rather than
raw scale. The pitch has shifted from "bigger" to "smarter and more trustworthy."

## 2. Compute economics dominated the hallway talk

Behind the keynotes, the recurring anxiety was cost. Who funds the next order of
magnitude of training compute — and whether the returns justify it — went
unresolved.

## 3. Regulators showed up prepared

Gone are the fumbling questions of prior years. Policymakers arrived fluent in
the vocabulary of evaluations, red-teaming, and model cards.

## 4. Enterprises want boring reliability

Buyers were blunt: they will trade a few benchmark points for predictability,
auditability, and support. Reliability sells.

## 5. Open and closed camps are converging on practice

However they license their weights, teams are settling on similar tooling for
evaluation, monitoring, and safety. The culture war cooled; the engineering
consensus grew.
