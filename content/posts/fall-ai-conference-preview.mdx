With the September launch blitz behind it, the AI world moves into conference
season — the stretch of research summits and industry gatherings that
traditionally close out the year. Here is what we'll be watching.

## From release notes to results

Launch week was about products; the fall calendar is where the research behind
them gets scrutinized. Expect deeper looks at reasoning robustness, evaluation
methodology, and safety — the themes that dominated the summer — presented as
papers rather than press releases. It is where the launch-week capability claims
either earn independent backing or quietly go unmentioned.

## The policy track

After the "Pacing the Frontier" statement and the summer's security incident,
governance is no longer a side session. Watch for concrete proposals on
incident disclosure, testing standards, and international coordination to move
from panels into draft frameworks. The interesting signal will be whether the
lab leaders who signed the letter say the same things on stage that they wrote on
paper.

## The money track

Conference season is also where budgets and bets get made in the hallways —
compute commitments, funding rounds, and partnerships that rarely make the
keynote but shape the following year. With prices falling and capability
clustering, expect hard questions about the economics underneath the demos.

## What we're listening for

Three questions will frame our coverage: Are the frontier capability claims from
launch week holding up under independent evaluation? Is the price war
sustainable or a temporary land-grab? And does any of the governance talk
translate into rules with teeth? We'll report from the season as answers emerge.
