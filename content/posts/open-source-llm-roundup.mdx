The gap between open and closed models keeps narrowing. A fresh wave of
permissively licensed releases now matches last year's frontier on many
benchmarks — and, crucially, runs on hardware teams can actually afford.

For builders weighing control and cost against peak capability, the calculus is
shifting. Open weights mean no per-token bill, no vendor lock-in, and the
freedom to fine-tune on proprietary data behind your own firewall.

## What changed

Three things converged: better training recipes, higher-quality open datasets,
and aggressive efficiency work that squeezes more capability into fewer
parameters. The result is models small enough to self-host that would have
counted as frontier not long ago.

## The trade-offs

Open models still trail the very best closed systems on the hardest reasoning
tasks, and running them well demands real MLOps maturity. Someone has to own
serving, scaling, and evaluation.

## How to choose

The honest answer is: benchmark on your own workload. Public leaderboards are a
starting point, not a decision. For many teams, a capable open model plus solid
retrieval beats a frontier API on both cost and latency — for others, the ceiling
still matters more than the bill.
