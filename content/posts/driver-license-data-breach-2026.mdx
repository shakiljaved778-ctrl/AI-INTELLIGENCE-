One of the year's largest data exposures didn't come from a household-name app. A **verification vendor** confirmed that **more than 150 million driver's licenses** were stolen from its cloud — the kind of breach that reveals how much of your identity sits with companies you never knowingly signed up with.

Identity-verification firms are the plumbing behind "upload a photo of your ID" flows on countless sites — banks, marketplaces, age-gated services. That makes them a single point of failure holding uniquely sensitive documents: a driver's license bundles your name, address, date of birth, and photo in one record that, unlike a password, you can't rotate. Stolen credentials can be reset; a stolen license can't.

## The third-party problem

The breach underscores a structural weakness in modern data security: your exposure is only as strong as the least careful vendor in a chain you can't see. You trust a service; that service trusts a verifier; the verifier gets breached, and your documents are on the market — with no clear way for you to have prevented it.

Practical defenses are limited but real: freeze credit, be alert to identity-theft attempts, and treat any "verify your identity" request with fresh skepticism. The broader fix is regulatory and architectural — minimizing how much sensitive data these intermediaries retain in the first place.
