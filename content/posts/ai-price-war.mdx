The loudest story of launch week was capability. The more consequential one, for
anyone paying the bills, was price.

## Cheaper tokens, especially for agents

Anthropic held Claude Fable 5.1's headline rates steady but cut cached-input
reads from $1.00 to $0.25 per million tokens — a change it says makes typical
workloads roughly 25% cheaper and heavily agentic ones as much as 45% cheaper.
Google, meanwhile, pushed Gemini 3.8 Flash as frontier-adjacent capability at
workhorse-tier cost.

## Why cached reads matter

Agentic systems re-read large, mostly unchanging context on every step as they
plan, call tools, and check their work. Discounting those cached reads targets
the exact cost that has made ambitious agent deployments expensive. It is a bet
that the next wave of demand is agents, not chat.

The mechanics matter because they change what is economically feasible. A
workflow that loops twenty times over a big context was, until recently, priced
like a luxury. Cut the dominant cost line by 45% and a whole class of
"too-expensive-to-run" ideas moves into budget.

## A margin question the labs aren't answering

Aggressive price cuts also raise a question the labs are not eager to discuss:
how much of frontier inference is sold near, at, or below cost to win share?
Falling prices are unambiguously good for buyers today, but a land-grab funded by
investor patience is not the same as a durable market price. Buyers enjoying the
discounts should still design for portability.

## The strategic read

Cutting price alongside a capability bump — rather than raising it — signals
confidence and a scramble for developers. For buyers, the compounding effect of
falling per-token costs may matter more to the roadmap than any single benchmark
win, and it makes a multi-model strategy cheaper to run than ever.
