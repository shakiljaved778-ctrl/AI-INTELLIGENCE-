The AI funding market has split in two. Enormous checks keep flowing to a
handful of frontier labs and chip makers. For nearly everyone else, the bar has
risen sharply: investors now want proof, a clear customer need, and defensible
data or IP before they commit.

## The end of easy AI money

Two years ago, "we're an AI company" was close to a fundable thesis on its own.
No longer. With capable models now cheap and abundant, "we call an API" is not a
moat. Investors have learned that the durable value sits in distribution,
proprietary data, and measurable customer outcomes — not in access to a model
anyone can rent.

## Where the money is going

The rounds that close cluster around vertical AI with hard results —
healthcare operations, legal work, construction, finance, and regulated
enterprise workflows — plus the infrastructure underneath: compute, chips, and
robotics. The common thread is defensibility and measurable ROI, echoing the
enterprise-adoption story: the winners point AI at a concrete, valuable job.

## What founders should take from it

Build something that works, for a customer who pays, in a way rivals can't
trivially copy. Instrument the outcome. Then raise. The market is still huge —
but it now funds evidence, not adjectives. That's a healthier market than the
hype cycle that preceded it, even if it's a harder one to raise in.
