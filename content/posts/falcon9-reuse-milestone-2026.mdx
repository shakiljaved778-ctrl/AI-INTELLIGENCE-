The most radical thing about spaceflight in 2026 is how boring the milestones have become. A **Falcon 9 booster** reportedly logged a **35th flight** — a number that would have sounded absurd a decade ago, when rockets were largely single-use and each launch threw away its most expensive hardware into the ocean.

That routine is the revolution. Reusing a booster dozens of times doesn't just cut the sticker price of a launch; it builds the operational trust and cadence that make space a place you *use* rather than visit. When the vehicle is proven across 35 flights, launch stops being a high-drama event and starts being a schedule.

## Why it matters beyond rockets

Cheap, frequent, reliable access to orbit is the enabling layer for almost everything else happening above our heads — communications constellations, Earth observation, navigation timing, and the science instruments that ride along. Drive the cost of the ride down far enough and entire categories of missions that were once unaffordable become merely expensive.

The lesson generalizes past aerospace: the technologies that change the world are often the ones that turn a miracle into a chore. A 35th flight isn't a spectacle. It's infrastructure — and that's exactly why it matters.
