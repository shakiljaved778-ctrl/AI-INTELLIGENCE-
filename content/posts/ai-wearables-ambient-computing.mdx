The dream of ambient computing — a helpful intelligence that is simply *present*,
without a screen to stare into — is one of tech's most seductive and most
disappointing. Every few years a device promises to free us from the phone, and
every few years it mostly doesn't. In 2026 the pitch is back, louder than before:
a new wave of AI glasses, pins, and pendants that want to put a capable assistant
in the room with you rather than behind a slab of glass.

What's different this time is the thing behind the hardware. The earlier attempts
failed partly because the AI wasn't good enough — the assistant misheard, missed
context, and answered slowly, so the magic evaporated on contact. Today's models
are dramatically more capable: they can see what you see, hear what you hear,
reason about it, and respond conversationally. The hardware finally has something
worth wearing it for.

## Where glasses have the edge

Of the form factors, smart glasses have the most credible case. They sit where
your eyes and ears already are, can show you a little without demanding your full
attention, and can discreetly capture the context an assistant needs. Translating
a conversation in real time, identifying what you're looking at, or surfacing a
quiet reminder is genuinely useful — and genuinely hard to do as well on a phone
you have to pull out.

## The unsolved problems

The obstacles are the same ones that have always haunted wearables. Battery life
fights against capability and size. A camera on your face is a social and privacy
problem the people *around* you never agreed to. And the fundamental question
remains: is there a job important enough that people will wear a second device all
day to do it? Standalone AI gadgets have a brutal track record precisely because
the phone is so good and so present.

## The real contest

The likely winner is not a single hero device but a shift in where AI lives —
spread across the glasses, the earbuds, the watch, and the phone, surfacing
wherever makes sense. The companies with the ecosystems and the distribution have
the advantage; the startups have the ideas. Ambient computing has been five years
away for a decade. The models are finally ready. Whether we are willing to wear
them is the question this wave will answer.
