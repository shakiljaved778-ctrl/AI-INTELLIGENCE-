The paradox of enterprise AI in 2026: adoption is nearly universal, but returns
are not. By one McKinsey figure, **88% of companies use AI — yet only ~39% see
a measurable earnings impact.** The gap between using AI and profiting from it
is where the real story lives.

## What the winners do differently

The companies actually cashing in — where strategic agentic deployments have
reportedly averaged around **171% ROI**, and higher in the US — tend to follow
the same playbook:

- **Narrow scope.** One well-defined problem, not "AI everywhere."
- **Business-outcome KPIs.** Hours saved, cost per case, error rate — not
  "engagement with the AI."
- **Human escalation.** Automate the routine; route exceptions to people.
- **Workflow integration.** Build into existing systems, not as a side demo.
- **Production, not pilots.** Long-lived deployments that compound.

## Why so many miss

Most failures aren't about model quality; they're about deployment. Splashy
pilots that never reach production, vague success metrics, and "transformation"
mandates without a specific job to do all produce activity without earnings. The
technology is rarely the bottleneck — the operating discipline is.

## The bottom line

AI is not a magic multiplier applied to a whole company; it is a tool that pays
when pointed at a concrete, measurable job and kept in production. The
enterprises turning AI into profit — JPMorgan, Klarna, Walmart, and others —
look less like moonshots and more like operations teams doing the unglamorous
work well.
