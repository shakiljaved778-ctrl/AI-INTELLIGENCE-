Wall Street closed out a jittery week the way it started it — watching the Federal Reserve. Stocks steadied, with a **dip in oil prices** helping equity buyers halt a **four-day slide in the S&P 500**, but the calm was uneasy. The reason sits in the inflation data.

The latest **Consumer Price Index rose 0.4%**, in line with forecasts but up sharply from July's 0.1%. More telling was **core CPI**, which strips out food and energy: it **advanced 0.3%**, above the 0.2% consensus and above July's pace. Firmer core inflation is exactly what makes the Fed hesitate to ease — and this print pushed money markets to treat a **September move as an almost-done deal**.

## Why the tension

Markets don't fear rate decisions so much as *ambiguous* ones. A hot core reading narrows the Fed's room to maneuver and raises the stakes on every subsequent data point — labor prints, inflation expectations, and the commentary around them. Falling oil offered a convenient counterweight this week, tempering the inflation narrative just enough to let dip-buyers step in. But cheaper crude is a fragile hedge; it can reverse on a single supply headline.

## What to watch

The setup heading into the decision is a familiar one: an economy still generating enough inflation to keep policymakers cautious, and an equity market that has already priced a lot of good news. That combination tends to make indexes twitchy — small data surprises producing outsized single-day moves, as the four-day drop and quick rebound both showed.

For long-term investors the signal matters more than the noise: the disinflation story that powered much of the prior rally is proving stickier to finish than the early stages suggested. Until core inflation resumes a convincing decline, "higher for longer" remains the base case markets keep being forced to re-learn — and every CPI Wednesday will keep carrying this much weight.
