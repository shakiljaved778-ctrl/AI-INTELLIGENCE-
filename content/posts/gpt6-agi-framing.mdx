When OpenAI's president suggested GPT-6 Astra might one day be seen as the
arrival of artificial general intelligence, it made for a striking headline. It
is also a claim worth holding at arm's length.

## Capability is not generality

GPT-6 Astra may well be a genuine leap on coding, science, and cybersecurity.
But being excellent across many benchmarks is not the same as the open-ended,
self-directed competence "AGI" is supposed to name. Benchmarks are curated;
the world is not.

## The framing has costs

Loose AGI talk cuts both ways. It inflates expectations that the next quarter's
usage data will deflate, and it distorts the risk conversation — pulling
attention toward speculative superintelligence and away from the concrete
problems, like the summer's security incident, that are already here.

## A better yardstick

Judge these systems by what they reliably do on your work, not by the label
their makers reach for at launch. The interesting question isn't whether GPT-6 is
"AGI." It's whether it fails less, on the tasks you actually care about, than
what you're running today. That's measurable — and far more useful.
