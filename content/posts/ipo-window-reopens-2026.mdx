After years of companies staying private and bankers staring at an empty pipeline, the IPO market is showing a pulse. **Goldman Sachs** projects U.S. IPO proceeds could **quadruple to roughly $160 billion** — a range it pegs from $80 billion to $200 billion — with the number of offerings **doubling to nearly 120**.

A reopening window matters beyond Wall Street's fee machine. IPOs are how private-market value becomes public, how employees and early backers get liquidity, and how ordinary investors get access to companies that spent their high-growth years off-limits. When the window is shut, that whole cycle stalls; when it opens, capital starts moving again.

## The caveats

Forecasts are not deals, and the spread here is telling — an $80 billion-to-$200 billion range is less a prediction than an acknowledgment that sentiment can swing hard on a few marquee listings and the direction of interest rates. A reopened window can slam shut on one bad print or one high-profile flop.

There's also the notable holdout: OpenAI's Sam Altman said the company won't go public in 2026, citing safety. When the most-watched private company in the world sits out, it tempers the "everyone's rushing to list" narrative. Still, after a long freeze, even a partial thaw changes the mood — and the calendar.
