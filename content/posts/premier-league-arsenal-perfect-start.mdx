Early September is when Premier League tables start telling small truths, and this weekend's told a clear one: Arsenal are setting the pace, and almost everyone else is leaving points on the table.

Mikel Arteta's side kept their **perfect start** intact with a controlled **2-0 win over Sunderland**, the kind of unfussy result that title campaigns are quietly built on. Behind them, the chasing pack wobbled. **Liverpool were held to a goalless draw by Fulham at Anfield** — a reminder that even the deepest squads run into stubborn, well-drilled visitors. **Chelsea** were pegged back to a **2-2 draw by newly promoted Hull City**, and **Tottenham** and **Everton** cancelled each other out in a **0-0**.

## The mid-table stories

Below the top, the weekend delivered the chaos the league sells itself on. **Ipswich Town edged Crystal Palace 3-2** in a five-goal swing of momentum, while **Nottingham Forest beat Aston Villa 2-1**, leaving Unai Emery's Villa still hunting a first league win — an ominous early hole for a side with European ambitions. **Bournemouth and Brentford** split a **2-2** that flattered neither defense.

## Why it matters

It's easy to overweight September, but dropped points now compound later. Arsenal's edge isn't the scoreline so much as the pattern: they're converting the exact fixtures — a home game against a newly promoted side — that rivals are drawing. Over a 38-game season, that difference between "win the games you should" and "draw a few of them" is usually the gap between lifting the trophy and finishing second.

For Villa, the concern is less tactical than psychological: winless starts have a way of turning process questions into confidence questions. And for the promoted clubs — Hull taking a point at Chelsea, Ipswich winning a thriller — the early message is that this season's newcomers intend to be awkward company.

Nothing is decided in week-something of a long season. But the shape of it is beginning to show, and right now it points north London.
