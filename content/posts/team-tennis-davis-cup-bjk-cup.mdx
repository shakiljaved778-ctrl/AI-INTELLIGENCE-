Tennis spends most of the year as a solitary pursuit, but September hands it over to the teams. The **Davis Cup Qualifiers** (Sept. 18-20) and the **Billie Jean King Cup Finals** in **Shenzhen** (Sept. 22-27) shift the sport from individual ambition to national duty — and change how the same players behave on court.

Team tennis is a strange, compelling format precisely because it inverts the usual incentives. A player who guards energy carefully across a long individual season will empty the tank for a doubles rubber that means nothing to their ranking and everything to their country. The crowds are partisan in a way the tours rarely are, and a captain's tactical choices — who plays, in what order — carry outsized weight.

## Why it resonates

For all the debate over scheduling and format tweaks these competitions have endured, the appeal is durable: watching individualists submit to a collective, and seeing nerves that a solo match rarely exposes. A missed break point in a Grand Slam is a personal setback. The same miss with teammates watching from the bench is something else.

The finals in Shenzhen also continue tennis's deepening footprint in Asia — a commercial as well as sporting story. But on court, the draw is simpler: the rare weeks when the loneliest sport becomes a team game.
