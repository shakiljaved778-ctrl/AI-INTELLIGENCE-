The idea of an international body to oversee frontier AI — long floated by
industry figures including OpenAI's and Google DeepMind's leadership — is gaining
traction as national governments struggle to keep pace with the technology.

## Why national rules aren't enough

Frontier models are trained and deployed globally. A rule that binds one country
can be sidestepped by moving compute or incorporation elsewhere, which is why
advocates argue that meaningful pacing requires coordination across borders — the
way arms control or aviation safety does.

## The "Pacing the Frontier" throughline

The argument runs directly through the "Pacing the Frontier" statement, in which
more than 1,200 lab employees asked the US government to support an international
effort to build the technical and governance tools needed to deliberately pace
automated AI development. Notably, the ask is to build the capacity to
coordinate — not to slow down today.

## The obstacles

International coordination is slow, and AI competition is entangled with national
security and economic advantage. Skeptics doubt rivals will cede any edge. But
the fact that the people building these systems are the ones calling for
guardrails gives the watchdog debate unusual momentum.
