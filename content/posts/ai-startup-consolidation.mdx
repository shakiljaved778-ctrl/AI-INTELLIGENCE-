Every technology gold rush has two phases: the frenzy, when money and startups
pour in, and the consolidation, when the field thins into a smaller set of winners.
After a few years of frenzy, AI is entering the second phase. The landscape is
consolidating — through outright acquisitions, through talent raids that hollow out
startups, and through the quiet shutdowns that rarely make headlines — and the shape
of the industry for the next decade is being decided now.

The dynamics driving it are familiar. Building at the frontier is extraordinarily
expensive, and the capital and compute required favor a handful of deep-pocketed
players. Many startups raised on enormous expectations, and as the market matured
and investors turned from growth-at-any-cost to a demand for durable business, some
could not justify their valuations or fund their next training run. For a promising
team in that position, being acquired is often the rational exit.

## The acqui-hire era

A distinctive feature of this cycle is how much of it runs on people. In a field
where a small group of researchers can move the frontier, acquiring a company is
frequently a way to acquire its talent — the "acqui-hire" raised to strategic art.
Some deals look less like buying a product than buying a team and quietly winding
down what it built. For the engineers, it can be a windfall; for the ecosystem, it
concentrates expertise further into the largest labs.

## What's lost and what's gained

Consolidation is not purely a loss. It can rescue good technology from failing
businesses, give ambitious ideas the resources to actually ship, and spare the
market a thousand near-identical products. But it also narrows the field, reduces
the number of independent bets, and tightens the grip of a few incumbents on a
foundational technology — exactly the concentration that makes regulators and
customers uneasy.

## What to watch

The tell will be where the survivors cluster: whether a durable layer of
independent companies emerges in the application layer, built on top of the big
labs' models, or whether value keeps flowing to the handful of players who own the
models and the compute. The frenzy built the field. The consolidation is deciding
who gets to keep it.
