Anthropic opened a frantic week of frontier releases on September 1 with
**Claude Fable 5.1** (alongside a companion model, Mythos 5.1), positioning it as
its strongest system yet for coding, knowledge work, and long-running agent
tasks — and making it available to all users on day one.

The most notable change is on price. Anthropic held the headline API rates steady
from Fable 5 but cut cached input reads from $1.00 to $0.25 per million tokens.
The company says that makes typical workloads roughly 25% cheaper, and heavily
agentic workloads — which lean on large, reused context — as much as 45% cheaper.

## Optimized for agents

The pricing move is a tell about where the market is heading. Agentic systems
re-read the same large context repeatedly as they plan, call tools, and check
their work, so cheaper cached reads translate directly into lower costs for the
exact workloads Anthropic is courting.

Pairing a capability bump with a cost cut — rather than a price increase — is an
aggressive stance in a week when every major lab was competing for the same
developers' attention. It also reflects a bet that the next wave of demand is
autonomous, tool-using agents rather than one-off chat completions, and that
whoever makes those cheapest to run captures the most volume.

## What Mythos adds

Shipping Fable 5.1 with a companion model, Mythos 5.1, gives teams a choice of
trade-offs within the same family — a pattern the labs increasingly favor, since
few real deployments want a single model for every task. Expect teams to route
cheaper, latency-sensitive calls to one tier and reserve the strongest model for
the hardest steps.

## The competitive backdrop

Fable 5.1 landed days ahead of OpenAI's GPT-6 Astra and Google's Gemini 3.8
Flash, part of a burst of releases that had buyers scrambling to re-benchmark.
For teams already standardized on Claude, the upgrade is close to free to adopt;
for everyone else, it sharpens the perennial question of capability versus cost —
and, this week, tilts it toward cost.
