The loudest call yet to slow down AI is coming from inside the house. Anthropic CEO **Dario Amodei** has published a roughly **3,800-word essay** urging companies and governments to "**pace the frontier**" of AI development — an unusual plea for restraint from someone helping to set that pace.

Amodei's argument isn't that progress should stop. It's that the industry should deliberately buy itself time and then, as he put it, make wise use of it. Progress, he wrote, would still feel fast. His starker warning was about what could go wrong if it doesn't slow: he cautioned that swarms of **rogue AI agents could take over the internet in as little as six months**.

## The resignation that lit the fuse

The essay didn't come from nowhere. It followed the very public resignation of a British Anthropic researcher, **Jacob Coxon**, who wrote in a series of posts on X that both Anthropic and OpenAI — where he had previously worked — are "**gambling with our lives**." Rather than distance himself, Amodei told CNN he agreed with Coxon "more than he disagreed."

That's a remarkable admission. A frontier-lab CEO publicly siding with a departing safety researcher's alarm — instead of downplaying it — signals that the internal debate over pace has broken into the open.

## From essay to policy asks

Crucially, Anthropic didn't stop at sentiment. Executives paired the essay with concrete **legislative and regulatory** requests: blocking the sale of the most advanced chips to adversarial nations, and enacting a **national law requiring safety testing of frontier models** before release. In other words, "pace the frontier" is being translated into rules that would apply to everyone — competitors included.

That framing matters, because voluntary restraint has an obvious flaw: whoever slows down first risks losing the race. Binding rules are how you make caution collective rather than suicidal. It's also, conveniently, a set of rules a well-resourced incumbent is better positioned to absorb than a scrappy challenger — a tension critics are already noting.

## Why it's resonating

The timing amplified everything. The plea landed the same week researchers documented AI agents being weaponized in real attacks and lawmakers in Washington signaled fresh appetite for oversight. For years, "slow down" was the message of outside critics. Now it's coming from the people building the models — and being backed with specific policy. Whether rivals actually ease off, or simply let Anthropic absorb the reputational credit while they keep shipping, is the question the next few months will answer.
