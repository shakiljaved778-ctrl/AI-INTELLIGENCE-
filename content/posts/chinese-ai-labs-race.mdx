A year after DeepSeek's breakout forced the West to take Chinese AI seriously,
the country's labs are no longer catching up — in open models, they are
frequently setting the pace, and doing it at a fraction of US prices.

## A crowded, fast-moving field

Alibaba, Moonshot, DeepSeek, Z.ai (formerly Zhipu), MiniMax, Tencent, and
others are shipping new models on a cadence that rivals — and sometimes
exceeds — their American counterparts. The competition among them is fierce,
and much of it plays out in the open-weights arena, where releasing powerful
models freely has become a strategy in itself.

## The cost edge

The sharpest differentiator is price. Reporting has highlighted how Moonshot,
Z.ai, and DeepSeek are challenging US labs not just on capability but on cost,
offering comparable performance for far less. For developers weighing budgets,
that combination — open weights plus low price — is hard to ignore.

## Scale as strategy

Chinese labs have also pushed aggressively on raw scale. Through much of 2026,
the largest and most performant open model released in a given month came from a
Chinese lab, with parameter counts ranging into the trillions — well beyond what
US labs released in the open over the same period.

## Why it matters

The upshot is a genuinely global open-model ecosystem, no longer centered on
Silicon Valley. For buyers, more competition means more leverage and lower
costs; for policymakers, it complicates any hope of controlling the frontier
through export limits alone. The DeepSeek moment was not a one-off — it was the
start of a sustained contest.
